# StockSense AI - 5-Minute Pitch Script (Shooting Script)

### [0:00 - 0:30] ON CAMERA
*(Visual: Face only. Directly to camera, conversational pace.)*

"Hi, I'm presenting StockSense AI, a predictive risk manager built for Track 2 of the Razorpay AI Builder Internship. 

In modern fintech, machine learning models spit out point predictions all the time. A price target, or a binary buy or sell signal. Downstream systems often treat those outputs as absolute certainties. But models are never certain. They have varying levels of confidence, they disagree with one another, and their accuracy degrades when the market enters volatile regimes. 

A real risk manager doesn't just ask 'what is the prediction?'. They ask 'how much should I trust this prediction right now?' That is the exact problem StockSense AI solves."

### [0:30 - 1:15] SCREEN SHARE
*(Visual: Show the Architecture Diagram slide detailing the 12 base models, the regime gate, and the portfolio aggregator. No camera bubble needed yet.)*

"To build a risk manager, I first needed a robust forecasting engine to manage. I built a comprehensive pipeline covering three major Indian equities: L&T, TCS, and Reliance. 

For each asset, the system trains 12 distinct models spanning Random Forests, XGBoost, and PyTorch LSTMs, evaluating both regression price targets and directional classification. This is trained and rigorously validated on 15 years of daily market data. 

The models achieve solid baseline performance. For example, the XGBoost regression model hits a 57.16 RMSE on L&T, and the LSTM hits 37.06% directional accuracy on Reliance, safely outperforming the majority class baseline."

### [1:15 - 2:30] SCREEN SHARE (webcam bubble in corner)
*(Visual: Show a clean slide of the `per_ticker_selection.md` table showing which models actually won per asset.)*

"But a prediction is just step one. Here is the actual AI Risk Manager architecture. 

First, I implemented a Logistic Regression meta-learner stack trained on out-of-fold predictions to avoid data leakage. Interestingly, the stack only reliably beat the base models for L&T classification. For TCS, the stack actually underperformed its own inputs, which I verified was a genuine generalization failure, not a data bug. For Reliance, a standalone LSTM performed best. Instead of forcing a complex architecture, I built a dynamic routing system that objectively serves the best architecture per-asset based on a strict 0.5% improvement margin.

Next is the Risk-Scoring Layer. An Isolation Forest monitors rolling 20-day volatility to detect anomalous regimes. If the market is calm, risk relies on empirical Value-at-Risk and model disagreement. If the gate triggers, risk scales aggressively. 

Finally, every prediction routes through an Explainability Layer. Depending on the dynamically served model, it extracts SHAP values natively for tree models, reconstructs weighted SHAP for the stack, or uses a high-fidelity Decision Tree surrogate proxy for the LSTM."
